    'An enterprise AI copilot strategy should start with workflows, not model names. The best first use cases are frequent, expensive, repetitive, and measurable: support triage, internal knowledge search, sales research, document review, code assistance, compliance prep, or operations reporting.',
    'A practical copilot roadmap ranks each use case by business value, data readiness, permission complexity, user adoption risk, and evaluation difficulty. This prevents teams from spending months on flashy demos while easier, higher-return workflows wait in the backlog.',
    'The strongest use cases also have clear success metrics. Before building, define what good looks like: shorter resolution time, fewer manual handoffs, faster onboarding, higher answer accuracy, lower ticket volume, or reduced engineering toil. Without baseline metrics, every pilot becomes a debate about vibes.',
    'Data access is the next constraint. A copilot can only help if it reaches the systems people already use, but broad access creates risk. Design around least-privilege connectors, scoped retrieval, role-aware answers, human approval for sensitive actions, and audit logs that show what the assistant saw and did.',
    'Adoption depends on fitting into existing work. A copilot buried in a separate portal will be ignored. The better pattern is to place assistance where decisions already happen: the desktop workspace, support console, repository, CRM, document system, or internal chat channel.',
    'PAVii.AI helps teams turn enterprise AI copilot ideas into production systems by combining workflow discovery, model routing, MCP integrations, evaluation harnesses, and permission design. The result is not another AI experiment. It is a measurable assistant that earns its place in daily operations.'
